About

I studied English Literature at Universitas Dr. Soetomo, Surabaya, where my research kept circling back to one question: how do stories build a self, and what happens to that self when the story is a live-service video game instead of a novel. My undergraduate thesis read nihilism through Final Fantasy XIV: Endwalker's Meteion using Nietzsche's philosophy, and I've extended that same lens in a follow-up journal article. Alongside that, I've co-authored six further papers with peers across literary, film, and digital-culture analysis.

Alongside research, I've spent over ten years doing freelance client services for an online gaming community and part-time game streaming with real-time audience engagement — so the games I write about are ones I also play and talk to other players about, not just texts I studied from a distance.

Undergraduate Thesis, Universitas Dr. Soetomo 2024

Nihilism Represented by Meteion in Naoki Yoshida's Final Fantasy XIV: Endwalker

Reads the character Meteion through Nietzsche's philosophy of nihilism, tracing three stages — recognition, systematization, and disbelief in the “true world” — across her arc. Argues that her transformation dramatizes the Übermensch: confronting the meaninglessness of existence and choosing, instead of despair, to forge self-created values.

Übermensch Delineated by Meteion in Naoki Yoshida's Final Fantasy XIV: Endwalker

A close reading of Meteion's arc as a dramatization of Nietzsche's Übermensch: her progression from despair toward self-improvement and self-created values, read against Nietzsche's Thus Spoke Zarathustra and The Will to Power. Argues that Endwalker's narrative doubles as a critique of nihilism, showing how a live-service game can carry serious philosophical weight.

Pioneer: Journal of Language and Literature 2023

The Language of Cyber Gender Anonymity on Hololive Virtual YouTubers

Reads Hololive VTubers' unreal names and avatars as a language of anonymity, using Judith Butler's account of gender as performance and Philip Zimbardo's deindividuation theory. Analyzes real doxxing cases (Kureiji Ollie, Kiryu Coco, Mano Aloe) to argue that this anonymity is inherently unstable — laws can protect a VTuber's identity, but not their psychological safety once viewers go looking for the person behind the avatar.
